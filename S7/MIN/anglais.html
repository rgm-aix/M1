Topic 1 · Fiche 1

What is tort law?

Un tort = un préjudice fautif (civil wrong). Le droit des torts est une branche du droit civil (l’autre : le contrat). Le demandeur obtient de l’argent, jamais de prison.

Definition & 3 types of torts

A tort is a wrongful injury. Both people and companies can commit torts.

  • Intentional: the wrongdoer acts deliberately (intentionnel)
  • Negligent: injures another through carelessness (négligence)
  • Reckless: conduct so extreme and outrageous that the likelihood of injury is very high, without intent to injure (imprudence grave)
TypeExample
Negligencea driver not paying attention carelessly drives through a stop sign
Recklessnessa drunk driver, texting, drives through a stop sign
Intentional ≠ intended consequencesA taps B’s head playfully; B has a fragile skull and needs surgery. The act (tap) was intentional, so the consequences fall on A.

Which type of tort? (Exercise B)

  • punches someone → battery
  • amputates the wrong leg → medical malpractice
  • unsafe conditions kill a worker → wrongful death
  • spreads vicious lies about a rival → defamation (libel written / slander spoken)
  • locks someone in a closet → false imprisonment

Autres : trespass, nuisance, fraud, invasion of privacy, product liability.

Civil law vs criminal law
CriminalCivil (tort)
Who sues?The government: The People v. XThe injured person (plaintiff)
AccusedDefendantDefendant (tortfeasor)
AimPunish a harm to the communityCompensate the victim
SanctionPrison, probationMoney (damages), injunction; never jail
Standard of proofBeyond a reasonable doubtPreponderance of the evidence
Some acts are both crimes and torts (assault, battery). Only the government can send you to jail. O.J. Simpson: acquitted of murder (criminal) but liable for wrongful death (civil). Not “double punishment”: two different purposes and standards.

Même sans poursuite pénale (pas de témoin indépendant), la victime peut demander réparation au civil.

Burden of proof & the 4 elements of negligence
  • The plaintiff carries the burden of proof; the defendant need not disprove anything.
  • Standard: preponderance of the evidence = more probable than not.
ElementMeaning
1. Dutyduty of care owed to the plaintiff (obligation de prudence)
2. Breach of dutyfailed to meet the standard of care (negligent, reckless, intentional)
3. Causationthe breach caused the injury
4. Damagesreal injury or loss suffered
Damage vs damagesDamage (uncountable) = harm or loss. Damages (always plural) = sum of money awarded. “Damages have been awarded”, “to suffer damage”.
Three benefits & why money?
  • Compensation: make the victim “whole”.
  • Disclosure: jury trials are public; evidence reveals hazardous practices (public safety).
  • Deterrence: verdicts force companies to stop dangerous practices or redesign products.

Why money? The only practical way to repair what cannot be undone, and it deters. Limits: a scar or a death cannot truly be priced; a rich defendant may pay and carry on. Punitive damages only punish, in limited cases.

À retenir
  • Tort = civil wrong ; 3 types : intentional, negligent, reckless
  • Criminal : l’État poursuit, prison ; civil : la victime poursuit, argent
  • Duty → Breach → Causation → Damages ; preuve par « preponderance of the evidence »
  • Damage (préjudice) ≠ damages (dommages-intérêts)

McDonald’s cases & Tort reform

Deux affaires contre McDonald’s et un débat politique : faut-il limiter les procès et les dommages-intérêts ?

A. The Hot Nugget case (2019 / 2023)

Facts: 2019, drive-thru, Tamarac (Florida). Olivia Caraballo, 4, got a second-degree burn on her thigh from a nugget from her Happy Meal. Her parents sued McDonald’s USA and the franchisee Upchurch Foods. Both sides agreed the nugget caused the burn; they disagreed on temperature (200°F+ vs max 160°F).

Plaintiffs’ teamDefendant’s team
Duty of care to children (a Happy Meal is made for kids)Food safety rules require hot cooking (bacteria, salmonella)
Serving dangerously hot food without warning = breachRisk is obvious: fresh food is hot
Foreseeable: eaten in the car straight from the drive-thruBeyond restaurant’s control after the window; parent should check (comparative fault)
Pain, permanent scar, emotional harm; Liebeck (1994) already warned McDonald’sHealed in 3 weeks: damages must stay reasonable ($156,000 vs $15 million asked)

Outcome: May 2023 jury: liable. July 2023: $800,000 ($400,000 past + $400,000 future pain and suffering).

Would you take the case? Yes: child victim, clear liability, sympathy, may change practices. No: quick healing = low damages, may look “frivolous”, long and costly. Contingency fee: US lawyer paid a share (~⅓) of damages, nothing if they lose.
B. Stella Liebeck v. McDonald’s (1994)
FactsPlaintiffDefendantOutcome
Feb 1992, Albuquerque. Passenger in parked car, spilled coffee while removing the lid. 3rd-degree burns on 6% of body, 8 days in hospital, skin graftsStella Liebeck, 79. First asked $20,000 for medical costs; refusedMcDonald’s Corporation: coffee at 180–190°F; 700+ burn complaints in 10 years; cup text was a “reminder”, not a “warning”McDonald’s 80% at fault. $200,000 compensatory → $160,000. $2.7M punitive → $480,000. Then secret settlement
  • Comparative negligence: plaintiff partly at fault (20%) → damages reduced proportionally.
  • Remittitur: judge cuts an excessive award (3× compensatory), while calling the conduct “reckless, callous and willful”.
  • Coffee at 155°F would have cooled and spared her a serious burn; at 180°F a full-thickness burn takes 2–7 seconds.
  • Media myth: she was not driving, the car was parked. Not a “frivolous” case.

Issues raised: unreasonably dangerous product? negligent or reckless? comparative fault? fair punitive damages? frivolous suit? need for tort reform?

C. Tort reform (HBO Hot Coffee)

Tort reform = changing the law to limit lawsuits and damages: caps on punitive and non-economic damages, stricter class actions, mandatory arbitration. Its supporters denounce “frivolous lawsuits”.

  • Origin: pressure from big corporations in the 90s; lobbies in Washington seeking federal legislation limiting consumers’ access to court.
  • PR strategy: a fake grassroots “citizen group” actually backed by a tobacco company; the Liebeck case used as a symbol.
  • Clinton vetoed a restrictive bill (1996): it “could have a devastating impact on innocent Americans”.
  • George W. Bush, “Tort Reformer in Chief”: caps on punitive, non-economic and total damages.
Economic damagesNon-economic damages
Specific costs: medical bills, property repairEmotional distress, pain and suffering
Who benefits? Companies, insurers, some doctors (lower payouts and premiums). Who loses? Injured plaintiffs (less compensation, less access to a jury). Vocabulary: a cap on damages (plafonnement), frivolous lawsuit (procès abusif), mandatory arbitration, to veto a bill.
À retenir
  • Nugget 2023 : $800 000 ; Liebeck 1994 : 80 % McDonald’s, $160k + $480k punitifs, puis accord secret
  • Comparative negligence = réduction selon la part de faute ; remittitur = juge réduit les dommages
  • Tort reform = soutenue par les entreprises (tabac), vetoée par Clinton, caps sous Bush

The Oscars slap — Rock v. Smith (2022)

Le 27 mars 2022, Will Smith gifle Chris Rock en direct (94th Academy Awards). Rock n’a jamais déposé de plainte réelle : la plainte étudiée est une hypothetical complaint (Superior Court of California, LA County).

Three causes of action
CauseWhat Rock must prove
BatterySmith intentionally touched him; contact harmful or offensive; no consent; harm
AssaultSmith’s conduct made him reasonably apprehend imminent harmful contact; he was aware; damage. No contact required
IIED (intentional infliction of emotional distress)Conduct extreme and outrageous; intentional or reckless; severe emotional distress
  • Assault = the threat (Smith walking toward Rock); battery = the contact (the slap).
  • IIED covers the psychological harm: humiliation before millions, reputational damage; supports punitive damages.
Damages & procedure
  • Compensatory: actual losses. General: pain, distress, humiliation (not precisely quantifiable). Punitive: punish a willful, malicious act and deter.
  • Complaint ends with a demand for jury trial; “realleges paragraphs 1–13” = restates earlier allegations by reference.
  • Smith = a public figure (higher standard in some claims, e.g. defamation).
  • Smith’s TV interview (Trevor Noah) can be used as evidence (“just lost it”, “rage” → intent), but he also said emotions did not justify it and expressed regret.
True / False corrections
  • Rock never filed a lawsuit or a police report.
  • The complaint brings three causes of action, not one.
  • Assault does not require contact (battery does).
  • Smith won Best Actor (King Richard) the same night; he resigned from the Academy and apologised in July 2022.
  • A confidentiality clause keeps the terms secret. Punitive damages punish; compensatory damages cover real losses.
Settlement & apology

Why settle? Faster, cheaper, certain, confidential, no admission of liability; both protect their image. Celebrities avoid trials (publicity, private life).

  • Public apology: eases anger but does not erase the right to compensation.
  • “No admission of liability”: mainly protects the defendant (pays without admitting wrongdoing).
  • Media focus on Smith’s tears can shift sympathy from the victim to the aggressor.
ClauseModel wording
1. PaymentSmith agrees to pay Rock $X within 30 days of signing
2. ConfidentialityThe Parties will keep the terms confidential
3. No admissionThis Agreement is not an admission of liability
4. ReleaseRock releases Smith from all claims and dismisses any action with prejudice
5. AdditionalPublic apology; no negative public statements
6. Governing lawState of California
À retenir
  • Battery (contact) ≠ assault (crainte d’un contact imminent) ≠ IIED (détresse émotionnelle)
  • Hypothetical complaint : Rock n’a jamais poursuivi ; règlement privé typique
  • Dismissal with prejudice = impossible de ressaisir la justice pour la même demande

O.J. Simpson: One Case, Two Verdicts

1995 : not guilty of murder. 1997 : liable for wrongful death. Trois questions-clés pour l’oral : le verdict, le racisme systémique, la charge de la preuve. Préparer 3 minutes sur chacune.

Criminal (1995) vs civil (1997)
Criminal 1995Civil 1997
Who brings the case?State of California (prosecutors)Victims’ families (plaintiffs)
PlaceDowntown Los AngelesSanta Monica
Jury9 Black jurors out of 12 (10 women)Mostly white
Standard of proofBeyond a reasonable doubtPreponderance of the evidence
Did Simpson testify?No (Fifth Amendment)Yes (could be forced)
DeliberationAbout 3 hoursAbout 17 hours
VerdictNot guilty (3 Oct 1995)Liable, unanimous; $33.5M damages
Q3: the prosecution must prove guilt; the defense only needs to raise a reasonable doubt. Burden of proof is not the only reason: different jury, place, testimony, deliberation.
Q1 — Delivering a verdict
  • Prosecution (Marcia Clark): motive via history of domestic violence (jurors saw no link to the murders); DNA and a blood trail; the bloody gloves.
  • Defense “Dream Team” (Cochran, Scheck): police had no search warrant and invented a pretext; motion to suppress the glove denied; badly handled DNA; glove demo: “If it doesn’t fit, you must acquit!”
  • Mark Fuhrman: detective who found the glove; tapes revealed racist remarks; he had sworn he never used the “n-word” (perjury) and took the Fifth Amendment. If you distrust the messenger, can you trust the evidence?
  • Trial: 133 days, 150 witnesses, 3 hours of deliberation. “Not guilty” ≠ “innocent”: the verdict says guilt was not proven beyond reasonable doubt, not a statement of truth.
  • Juries: right to jury trial in criminal and civil cases (Constitution, Bill of Rights). Founding Fathers: old and direct form of government, all citizens equal, power belongs to the people, protected from corruption.
Q2 — Systemic racism
  • Context: March 1991 Rodney King beaten by LAPD · Latasha Harlins (15) shot, shop owner gets probation · April 1992 acquittal of the officers, riots, 60+ dead · Oct 1995 verdict.
  • Verdict celebrated by many Black Americans, condemned by many white Americans. “Was the jury judging Simpson or the LAPD?” Possible jury nullification (acquittal rejecting the system despite the evidence).
  • Playing the “race card”: turning the trial into a trial of the LAPD.
  • June 1994 covers: Newsweek and TIME used the same mug shot; TIME darkened his skin (“An American Tragedy”) to look more threatening; the magazine apologised. Legal issues: presumption of innocence, burden of proof, media influence on the jury, media and race.
À retenir
  • Same man, two verdicts : preuve « beyond a reasonable doubt » (pénal) vs « preponderance » (civil), plus jury et lieu différents
  • Fuhrman, gants, ADN mal traité : le doute raisonnable
  • Verdict lu à travers LAPD, King, Harlins, émeutes de 1992
