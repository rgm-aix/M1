Fiche #1 · Topic 1 — Tort law and compensation culture

Tort law — Les bases (Reference sheet)

Un tort est un préjudice fautif. Le droit de la responsabilité délictuelle (civil law) permet à la victime d'obtenir de l'argent ; il ne mène jamais en prison.
🇬🇧 English Part — Study Sheet
1 What is a tort?
Definition & three types

A tort is a wrongful injury. Both people and companies can commit torts.

  • Intentional: the wrongdoer acts deliberately
  • Negligent: the wrongdoer injures another through carelessness
  • Reckless: conduct so extreme and outrageous that, even with no intent to injure, the likelihood of injury is very high
ExamplesNegligence: a driver not paying attention drives through a stop sign. Recklessness: a driver under the influence of alcohol and texting drives through a stop sign.

Other torts: battery, false imprisonment, medical malpractice, defamation (libel/slander), wrongful death, trespass, nuisance, fraud, product liability. A company that injures many people can face a class action.

2 Civil law vs criminal law
Criminal lawTort law (civil)
Who sues?The government (“The People v. …”)The injured person (plaintiff)
AimPunishment, injury to the communityCompensation for injuries and losses
SanctionPrison / probationMoney (damages) — never jail
Standard of proofBeyond a reasonable doubtPreponderance of the evidence

Some acts are both crimes and torts (battery, assault). Example: O.J. Simpson was acquitted of murder in the criminal case but found liable in the civil case. Even without a criminal case (no witnesses), the victim can still sue.

3 Burden of proof & the 4 elements of negligence

The plaintiff has the burden of proof; the defendant does not need to disprove anything. If one element is missing, the plaintiff loses.

  • Duty of care: a duty to follow the rules / not to injure others
  • Breach of duty: failure to meet the standard of care (negligent, reckless or intentional)
  • Causation: the defendant caused the injury
  • Damages: real loss or injury
Damage vs damagesDamage (uncountable) = harm suffered. Damages (always plural) = money awarded. Punitive damages only punish; compensatory damages make the plaintiff “whole”.
4 Three benefits of tort law
  • Compensation: money to make the victim “whole”
  • Disclosure: public jury trials reveal dangerous practices or products
  • Deterrence: verdicts force companies to stop dangerous practices (safer toys, food, cars)

Why money? It is the only practical way to repair an injury, and it deters. Limit: a scar or a death cannot really be measured in money, and a rich defendant may simply pay and continue.

Exercise BPunches someone → battery · amputates the wrong leg → medical malpractice · unsafe conditions kill a worker → wrongful death · spreads vicious lies → defamation · locks someone in a closet → false imprisonment.
🇫🇷 Partie Française — Traduction & fiche de révision
1–4 Les bases
Définition et trois types
  • Un tort = un préjudice fautif (personnes comme entreprises)
  • Intentionnel : acte délibéré · Négligent : préjudice causé par imprudence · Imprudence grave (reckless) : comportement extrême, risque très élevé sans intention de nuire
  • Autres torts : coups et blessures (battery), séquestration (false imprisonment), faute médicale, diffamation, décès illicite, intrusion, nuisance, fraude, responsabilité du fait des produits. Plusieurs victimes → class action (action de groupe)
Pénal vs civil
  • Pénal : l'État poursuit (« The People v. … »), peine possible de prison, preuve au-delà du doute raisonnable
  • Tort : la victime (plaintiff) poursuit l'auteur (defendant) pour obtenir de l'argent, jamais de prison, preuve par prépondérance de la preuve (plus probable qu'improbable)
  • Certains actes sont les deux (battery, assault) ; la victime peut agir au civil même sans poursuite pénale
Charge de la preuve et 4 éléments
  • La charge de la preuve pèse sur le demandeur ; un élément manquant = il perd
  • Duty (obligation de diligence) · Breach (manquement) · Causation (lien de causalité) · Damages (préjudice)
  • Damage = le dommage · Damages = dommages-intérêts (toujours pluriel). Compensatory = réparent · punitive = punissent
  • 3 bénéfices : compensation, disclosure (transparence des procès), deterrence (dissuasion)
À retenir
  • Tort = civil : argent, pas de prison, preuve « plus probable qu'improbable »
  • 4 éléments : duty, breach, causation, damages
  • Damage (dommage) ≠ damages (dommages-intérêts)
  • Compensation, disclosure, deterrence
Fiche #2 · Topic 1 — Tort law and compensation culture

Cas McDonald's : le nugget (2019) et Stella Liebeck (1994)

Deux affaires de brûlures contre McDonald's. La seconde, souvent moquée comme « frivole », repose sur des faits graves et sur le comportement de l'entreprise.
🇬🇧 English Part — Study Sheet
A Case study #1 — The hot nugget
Facts

2019, drive-thru in Tamarac, Florida. Olivia Caraballo, 4, got a second-degree burn on her thigh from a Chicken McNugget (Happy Meal) that fell between her leg and the car seat. Her parents (Philana Holmes and Humberto Caraballo Estevez) sued McDonald's USA and the franchisee Upchurch Foods. Both sides agree the nugget caused the burn; they disagree on temperature (over 200°F for the family, max. 160°F for the defense).

Closing arguments
  • Plaintiffs: duty of care towards children · food too hot with no warning = breach · eating in the car was foreseeable · causation undisputed · damages: pain, permanent scar, emotional harm · McDonald's knew the risk (Liebeck)
  • Defense: food safety rules require hot cooking · the risk is obvious · after the window, the parent should have checked (comparative fault) · healed in 3 weeks, damages must be reasonable ($156,000 vs $15 million asked)
Outcome: 2023, McDonald's liable; the jury awarded $800,000 ($400,000 past + $400,000 future pain and suffering).

Would you take the case? Yes: a child, clear liability, jury sympathy, pushes change. No: quick healing, low damages, may look “frivolous”, long and costly. Lawyers work on a contingency fee (about a third of damages, nothing if they lose).

B Stella Liebeck v. McDonald's Corporation (1994)
FactsPlaintiffDefendantOutcome
Feb. 1992, Albuquerque. Passenger in a parked car, she spilled coffee on her lap removing the lid. Third-degree burns on 6% of her body, 8 days in hospital, skin graftsStella Liebeck, 79. Asked $20,000 for medical costs; McDonald's refusedServed coffee at 180–190°F; 700+ burn complaints in 10 years (1982–92)McDonald's 80% at fault. $200,000 compensatory → $160,000; $2.7M punitive → $480,000 (3×). Then secret settlement
  • Comparative negligence: plaintiff partly responsible → damages reduced (20% fault)
  • Remittitur: judge cuts an excessive jury award, while calling the conduct “reckless, callous and willful”
  • Damaging testimony: coffee “not fit for consumption” at that temperature; at 180°F a full-thickness burn takes 2–7 seconds; the cup text was a “reminder”, not a “warning”
  • Legal issues: negligence or recklessness? warning? fair punitive damages? frivolous lawsuit? media distortion? tort reform?
Vocabulary (fill-in)The plaintiff was Mrs Liebeck. She sued McDonald's, saying it had been negligent. The jury heard damaging testimony from witnesses, even those called by the defendant, and ruled she should receive compensatory and punitive damages.

A–B Traduction
Le nugget (2019)
  • Olivia, 4 ans, brûlure au 2e degré à la cuisse (nugget coincé dans le siège auto). Les parents poursuivent McDonald's USA et le franchisé Upchurch. Désaccord sur la température : +200°F (famille) vs 160°F max (défense)
  • Demandeurs : obligation de diligence envers les enfants · nourriture trop chaude sans avertissement = manquement · risque prévisible · causalité non contestée · préjudice : douleur, cicatrice, souffrance morale · McDonald's connaissait le risque (Liebeck)
  • Défense : normes d'hygiène · risque évident · le parent devait vérifier (faute concurrente) · guérison en 3 semaines : préjudice à limiter (156 000 $ contre 15 M$ demandés)
  • Issue : McDonald's responsable ; 800 000 $ (400 000 passé + 400 000 futur). Contingency fee = honoraires de résultat (≈ 1/3, rien si échec)
Liebeck (1994)
  • Février 1992, Albuquerque : Stella Liebeck, 79 ans, renverse un café en retirant le couvercle ; brûlures au 3e degré (6 % du corps), 8 jours d'hôpital, greffes. Demande 20 000 $, McDonald's refuse
  • McDonald's : café à 180–190°F, plus de 700 plaintes en 10 ans
  • Verdict : McDonald's responsable à 80 % ; 200 000 $ → 160 000 $ ; punitifs 2,7 M$ → 480 000 $ ; puis transaction secrète
  • Comparative negligence = faute partagée (réduction proportionnelle) · Remittitur = le juge réduit des dommages jugés excessifs
  • Vocabulaire : plaignante (plaintiff), poursuivre (sue), négligence, témoignage, témoins, défendeur, a statué (ruled), dommages compensatoires et punitifs
À retenir
  • Liebeck : 80/20, 160 000 $ + 480 000 $ (punitifs ×3), 700 plaintes, 180–190°F
  • Nugget : jury 2023, 800 000 $
  • Le café n'était pas « chaud » mais brûlant : l'affaire n'est pas frivole
Fiche #3 · Topic 1 — Tort law and compensation culture

Tort reform — Hot Coffee (HBO)

Des grandes entreprises ont fait campagne pour limiter les procès et les indemnités. Le cas Liebeck est devenu leur symbole.
🇬🇧 English Part — Study Sheet
C Tort reform
  • Meaning: changing the law to limit lawsuits and damages: caps on punitive and non-economic damages, stricter class action rules, mandatory arbitration; aimed at “frivolous” lawsuits
  • Who pushed it: big corporations (nervous in the 90s), as lobbies in Washington, to pass federal legislation limiting consumers' right to go to court
  • PR strategy: a “grassroots citizen group”… actually backed by a tobacco company; the Liebeck case was used as a symbol of excess
  • Bill Clinton: vetoed a restrictive bill passed by the Senate: it “could have a devastating impact on innocent Americans”
  • George W. Bush: “Tort Reformer in Chief” — passed caps (punitive, non-economic, total)
  • Who benefits: companies, insurers, some doctors (lower damages and insurance costs). Who loses: injured plaintiffs (less compensation, less access to a jury)
DamagesDefinition
EconomicSpecific costs: medical bills, repair of damaged property
Non-economicEmotional distress, pain and suffering, etc.
Damages capA law limiting the amount of non-economic damages in a case
Do you agree with Clinton? (personal opinion, to justify with Liebeck / the nugget case: caps protect companies, not victims; but frivolous suits are also a concern.)
🇫🇷 Partie Française — Traduction & fiche de révision
C Traduction
  • Tort reform : réformer le droit pour limiter les procès et les indemnités (plafonds sur les dommages punitifs et non économiques, action de groupe encadrée, arbitrage obligatoire) contre les procès « abusifs » (frivolous)
  • Pression des grandes entreprises (années 90) via des lobbies à Washington pour limiter le droit des consommateurs d'aller en justice
  • Campagne de RP : faux « groupe de citoyens » soutenu par une entreprise du tabac ; Liebeck brandi comme symbole
  • Clinton : veto contre une loi restrictive (« effet dévastateur sur des Américains innocents »)
  • G. W. Bush : « Tort Reformer in Chief », plafonds de dommages
  • Bénéficiaires : entreprises, assureurs, médecins · perdants : victimes
  • Dommages économiques (factures médicales, réparations) vs non économiques (souffrance, détresse morale) ; cap = plafond
À retenir
  • Tort reform = limiter procès et dommages ; poussée par les lobbies d'entreprises
  • Clinton oppose son veto ; Bush « Tort Reformer in Chief »
  • Economic vs non-economic damages
Fiche #4 · Topic 1 — Tort law and compensation culture

The Oscars slap — Rock v. Smith (2022)

Le 27 mars 2022, Will Smith gifle Chris Rock en direct. Aucune plainte réelle n'a été déposée : la plainte étudiée est hypothétique, rédigée pour le cours.
🇬🇧 English Part — Study Sheet
D Facts & causes of action

At the 94th Academy Awards, Smith slapped Rock over a joke about his wife's hair; he later said he “just lost it” (“a rage bottled for a long time”) while saying his emotions did not justify it. He won Best Actor (King Richard) that night, resigned from the Academy (banned 10 years) and apologised in July 2022. Rock never sued nor filed a police report.

Cause of actionWhat Rock must prove
BatteryIntentional touching, harmful or offensive, without consent, causing harm
AssaultConduct making him reasonably apprehend imminent harmful contact; he was aware; damages (no contact needed)
IIEDExtreme and outrageous conduct, intentional or reckless, causing severe emotional distress
  • Assault vs battery: assault = the threat (seeing Smith walk up); battery = the contact (the slap)
  • Why IIED: covers the humiliation before millions, reputational harm; may raise damages and support punitive damages
  • Damages: compensatory (actual losses) · general (pain, humiliation, not precisely quantifiable) · punitive (punish a willful, malicious act)
  • Why settle: faster, cheaper, certain, confidential, no admission of liability, protects both images
  • Evidence: Smith's TV interview could be used (“just lost it” suggests intent); the defense can stress his regret
  • Apology: does not erase the right to compensation. A “no admission of liability” clause mainly protects the defendant
Settlement agreement (example)

1. Payment: $500,000 within 30 days · 2. Confidentiality: will keep terms confidential · 3. No admission of liability · 4. Release of all claims; dismissal with prejudice · 5. Additional: public apology, no negative statements · 6. Governing law: California.

🇫🇷 Partie Française — Traduction & fiche de révision
D Traduction
  • Aux Oscars 2022, Smith gifle Rock après une blague sur la coiffure de sa femme ; il dit avoir « perdu le contrôle » tout en reconnaissant que ses émotions ne justifiaient rien. Meilleur acteur le même soir, démission de l'Académie, excuses en juillet 2022. Rock n'a jamais porté plainte.
  • Battery : contact intentionnel, nuisible ou offensant, sans consentement · Assault : menace, crainte raisonnable d'un contact imminent (pas de contact nécessaire) · IIED : infliction intentionnelle de détresse émotionnelle (conduite extrême et outrageante)
  • Assault = la menace · battery = la gifle. L'IIED couvre l'humiliation et l'atteinte à la réputation
  • Dommages : compensatoires (pertes réelles), généraux (souffrance, non chiffrable), punitifs (punir)
  • Pourquoi transiger : plus rapide, moins cher, résultat certain, confidentiel, sans reconnaissance de responsabilité
  • L'interview télévisée peut servir de preuve. Des excuses n'effacent pas le droit à réparation. La clause « no admission of liability » protège surtout le défendeur
  • Accord type : paiement (ex. 500 000 $ sous 30 jours) · confidentialité · pas d'aveu · renonciation aux recours, dismissal with prejudice (classement définitif) · clauses annexes (excuses) · droit californien
À retenir
  • 3 causes of action : battery, assault, IIED
  • Assault = apprehension ; battery = contact
  • Compensatory / general / punitive damages
  • Settlement : confidentiality, no admission, with prejudice
Fiche #5 · Topic 2 — Criminal and civil American law (vocabulaire à l'examen)

O.J. Simpson : one case, two verdicts

1995 : not guilty of murder. 1997 : liable for wrongful death. Trois questions pour l'oral : le verdict, le racisme systémique, la charge de la preuve.
🇬🇧 English Part — Study Sheet
Q1 Delivering a verdict
  • Prosecution (1995): motive (domestic violence, to break his image — jurors saw no link) · physical evidence: DNA, blood trail, bloody gloves
  • Defense “Dream Team”: no search warrant (police climbed the walls “to protect him”); motion to suppress denied · Fuhrman, a racist cop who may have planted the glove, took the Fifth after the tapes · Scheck: DNA badly handled · “If it doesn't fit, you must acquit!” (Cochran) · the “race card”: trial of the LAPD
  • Jury: trial moved to Downtown LA; 9 African-Americans, 1 Hispanic, 2 white; 10 women. 133 days, 150 witnesses, 3 hours of deliberation. Not guilty ≠ innocent
  • Trial by jury is in the Constitution and Bill of Rights (criminal and civil): old, direct, democratic, equal citizens, protected from corruption
Q2 Systemic racism
  • TIME vs Newsweek (1994): same mug shot; TIME darkened Simpson's skin to look more threatening → scandal, apology. Legal issues: presumption of innocence, burden of proof, media influence on the jury
  • LA context: Rodney King (1991) beaten by LAPD · Latasha Harlins (1991) shot, shop owner got probation · 1992 riots (60+ dead) after acquittal of officers · 1995 verdict celebrated by many Black Americans
  • Jury nullification: acquittal rejecting the law or system despite evidence
Q3 The burden of proof

Who brings itState of CaliforniaVictims' families
Place / juryDowntown LA · 9 Black jurorsSanta Monica · mostly white
StandardBeyond a reasonable doubtPreponderance of the evidence
Simpson testified?No (Fifth Amendment)Yes (could be forced to)

VerdictNot guiltyLiable (unanimous), $33.5 million
🇫🇷 Partie Française — Traduction & fiche de révision
Q1–3 Traduction
  • Accusation : mobile (violences conjugales, jurés non convaincus) · preuves matérielles : ADN, traînée de sang, gants ensanglantés
  • Défense : pas de mandat de perquisition ; requête d'exclusion rejetée · Fuhrman (policier raciste, a plaidé le 5e Amendement) · Scheck : ADN mal traité · « Si ça ne va pas, acquittez ! » · « race card » : procès de la police de Los Angeles (LAPD)
  • Jury : 9 Afro-Américains, 1 hispanique, 2 blancs, 10 femmes ; 3 h de délibération. « Not guilty » ≠ « innocent »
  • Couvertures TIME / Newsweek : peau assombrie → scandale. Présomption d'innocence, influence des médias
  • Contexte : Rodney King, Latasha Harlins, émeutes de 1992 ; verdict célébré par beaucoup de Noirs américains. Jury nullification = acquittement qui rejette la loi malgré les preuves
  • Pénal : au-delà du doute raisonnable ; civil : prépondérance de la preuve. 1997 : responsable à l'unanimité, 33,5 M$ ; Simpson a dû témoigner au civil
À retenir
  • 1995 not guilty (doute raisonnable) ≠ 1997 liable (prépondérance)
  • Fuhrman et le LAPD ont transformé le procès en procès du racisme
